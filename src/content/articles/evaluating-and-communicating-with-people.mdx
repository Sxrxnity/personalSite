## I: What Does Effective Communication Entail?

**First principles** - The purpose of communication (for the speaker) is to deliver 'something' to the receiver. If the
receiver successfully understands that 'something', that instance of communication was successful.

It's easy to visualise this if we imagine two people, one of whom speaks English and the other Spanish, trying to
compliment each other. They could be saying the nicest things in the world, but because neither can comprehend what the
other wants to express, their communication is not effective.

Ineffective communication becomes much harder to recognise when both parties speak the same 'language'. However, due to
a number of contextual factors such as culture and upbringing, the same language often differs in what its speakers
intend to convey with a particular word.

"Clean" to someone who was brought up on a farm might be vastly different from "clean" to someone who lives in a mansion
maintained by butlers. (Excuse the generalisations, but hopefully you get the sentiment I intend to express.)

The fact is that everyone speaking, say, English has their own shade, or perception, of what each word means. Most
people tend to believe that their perception is reality (sometimes consciously, sometimes subconsciously). This is not
inherently a flaw; in fact, it's how we as humans make sense of the world around us without having all the knowledge
required to fill in every single gap.

However, this is not good for effective communication, as it means we give a little too much importance to our own
perception, and a little less to others' perception. The "right" balance (if there even is one) of different
perceptions is much more of an art than it is a science, but it is a limit that we, who strive to be effective
communicators, must persistently reach for.

In essence, because we're trying both to understand what other people are trying to say and to get them to understand
what we wish to communicate, we must align ourselves with their language.

On the rare occasion you meet someone who is also a competent speaker and receiver, you'll find communication much
easier. You'll both be actively adapting your language to the other's, while translating theirs into your own.
