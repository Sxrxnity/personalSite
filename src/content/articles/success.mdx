There is a concept in Chinese called *dào* (道), which loosely translates to "the way". Within this word lie
the secrets of the universe, and philosophers across millennia have dedicated entire lifetimes to seeking it, in hopes
that understanding it will answer humanity's deepest questions, such as "what is the meaning of life?" and "what does it mean to live a successful one?"

I am by no means Socrates or Nietzsche, but I too have wondered endlessly about the meaning of life and questions like it.
I do not claim to have found a definitive answer to any of these questions, nor do I believe there is one. However, I
hope that what I share will spark an idea in your mind and help you get closer to answering these questions for
yourself.

This article focuses on the second of those questions: what is success? It is harder to answer than it seems, because
success is a multidimensional word, and limiting it to one shade would be disrespectful to its intricacies. Below are three perspectives, in each of
which success means something different. I'm sure that when you clicked on this article, you had already assigned a
certain 'meaning' or 'shade' to the word "success". Start with the perspective that most closely resembles the 'meaning'
in your mind.

## I: Evolutionary / Biological

Zoom out. Past yourself, past your neighbourhood, past your city, past your country, and consider humanity as a species.
The goal of any species is survival. The longer it survives, the more successful it is.

In that regard, humanity has been extremely successful. Through every major revolution, natural disaster, and war, our
species has endured. It is truly amazing how quickly humans adapt to any environment (for better or worse), and that
is one of the key reasons for our success as a species.

For you and me as individuals, this means that nothing we do matters (and I mean that in a liberating sense rather than
a negative one). It is impossible for one individual, alone, to bring about any meaningful change on a global scale. The
masses will decide the direction of the next era (though there are certainly ways to influence them).

Humanity, in all of its brilliant ingenuity, brutal selfishness, boundless compassion, blind cruelty, unyielding courage,
and crippling fear, was destined to go through every great and tragic event in our history that has
contributed to our evolution.

The average person nowadays lives a better life than an emperor from a millennium ago, and I'm certain the same will be
true a millennium from now. Compared to the grand scale of not just the evolution of humanity, but of the universe
and all matter that exists, we individuals are a microcosm of a microcosm.

The success of our species ironically depends on each and every one of us, and none of us, at the same time. Humanity
really is a paradox.

## II: Scientific / Technical / Career

This section can be split into two distinct yet related parts: expertise and accolades.

In your vocabulary, expertise might mean skill, ability, capability, or any other synonym. Expertise could entail
knowledge, depth, accuracy, speed, insight, etc. In any case, a true expert should be extremely well-versed in their
discipline(s), and this should become apparent within a few interactions.

Generally speaking, there is a positive correlation between expertise and accolades. The best athletes in the world
usually win the most competitions. The (for lack of a better word) "smartest" scientists in the world are usually
responsible for the majority of our scientific breakthroughs. Accolades can also include important positions, prestigious awards,
academic titles, publications, records, and rankings.

There are obviously different definitions of "success", but for a more measurable category like this one, there is
usually a clear, objective ranking with a definite ceiling: world number one.

In short: be the best at what you do.

## III: Human / Spiritual

As opposed to what one might call the "hard skills" of the previous section, this section revolves around "soft
(human) skills", e.g. introspection, understanding, sensitivity, and compassion — qualities that are often overlooked and
less cultivated than hard skills.

There are two hallmark signs of an emotionally mature person, the first of which is the ability to appreciate beauty.
Whether that's art, music, dance, or poetry, their sensitivity and ability to resonate with the experience the artist
wishes to convey allow them to feel the work as it was meant to be felt. In this way, they enjoy a broader, richer
range of human experience, as they can resonate with the experiences of others as if they were their own.

This beauty, most of the time, isn't extravagant or significant. Most people can appreciate great works of art to some extent. If anything, it is one's ability to be inspired by,
and find meaning in, the most seemingly mundane matters of life that demonstrates the depth of one's aesthetic sensibility.

The second hallmark of an emotionally mature person is their ability to be positive. I'm almost certain that at one
point or another, you've been introduced to the concept of 'growth mindset'. For a very long time, I treated those
words as an empty platitude. It wasn't until I finally **felt** the value of positivity that I started to incorporate it into
my life.

**Positivity requires much greater strength than negativity.**

When sh\*t really starts to feel bad, it is so easy to drown in our emotions instead of focusing on what we can do.
We relinquish our autonomy to external factors, not realising that within us lies the power to choose how we feel.
In that regard, a person who is positive a lot of the time must possess a certain level of mental fortitude that allows them to convert negative experiences into positive ones. They control how their environment makes them feel, as
opposed to the far more passive inverse.

Here, success isn't something you achieve; it's something you feel.
